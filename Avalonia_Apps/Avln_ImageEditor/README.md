# Avln Image Editor

The reusable Avalonia control and the standalone host implement a 1–16 pixel brush, eraser and straight line, solid flood fill, coordinate-based rectangular cropping, 90° clockwise and counterclockwise rotation, **Rotate 180°**, horizontal (left/right) and vertical (top/bottom) flipping, **Invert colors**, and **Convert to grayscale**, with undo/redo. Each operation is one undo step when it changes pixels; no-ops retain the redo branch. Edits never save automatically. This is not a full image editor. Select and zoom do not change pixels.

Transparent and partially transparent pixels are displayed over a subtle tiled checkerboard confined to the image bounds. The pattern scales and scrolls with the image, disappears when no image is loaded, and does not receive pointer input or become part of the image, history or PNG export. Its dark neutral colors match the editor's existing fixed dark surface.

**Fit (min 10%)** applies a one-time zoom based on the usable image scroll viewport in device-independent units and the image's pixel dimensions. The image scrollbars use `Visible` rather than `Auto`, so a theme with non-overlay scrollbars reserves their space even when the image fits; this keeps the viewport and repeated fits stable. A theme may instead display them as overlays. Fit does not automatically re-run after resizing. Bitmap DPI does not change the calculation. The result is limited to 10%–800%; at 10%, a sufficiently large image may still be clipped and require scrolling. The button is disabled without an image and does nothing for an unrealized, hidden or zero-sized viewport. Fitting changes neither image pixels nor crop coordinates, PNG output, dirty state or undo/redo history.

The shared crop panel accepts zero-based X (from the left) and Y (from the top), and positive width and height in pixels. A valid partial-image rectangle is outlined on the image at its pixel edges, following zoom and scroll; invalid inputs, a missing image, and the whole-image rectangle hide the outline. The contrasting outline is non-interactive and is not included in pixels, history or PNG export. Enter whole numbers or explicitly enable **Drag crop selection**, then press **Apply crop**. There is no resampling or padding. Invalid or out-of-bounds inputs show an error and disable the command, leaving pixels and history unchanged. **Reset crop coordinates** works even with invalid inputs and restores the full current image rectangle; it is disabled without an image and does not reset or modify the image, dirty state, history or export. Applying the whole image is a no-op and retains redo. Loading any image and every dimension change (including crop, rotation, undo and redo) reset all four fields to the full current image; same-size edits retain the fields. Typed coordinates are never silently clamped.

Drag mode is off initially and after a document revision or view-model change. With the mode on, press the left mouse button inside the image and drag in either direction; release may be outside the image and is clipped to its edges. Coordinates are transformed through the actual image surface, so zoom, scroll and bitmap DPI do not alter pixel mapping. Coordinates within `1e-9` pixel of an integer are snapped to that edge to avoid transform round-off; then the lesser edge is rounded down and the greater edge up, selecting every touched pixel. A click or zero-width/height line produces an invalid zero-area selection rather than retaining a previous crop. Apply is disabled throughout a gesture, including direct `ApplyCrop()` calls. Escape, capture loss, disabling the mode, detaching the control or a pixel operation cancels an active drag and restores the pre-drag inputs; document replacement discards it and resets coordinates. If another actor edits the fields during a drag, the gesture is cancelled and width is invalidated to prevent applying mixed coordinates. Only Apply changes pixels or history. Numeric inputs remain available as an alternative; this does not claim complete physical accessibility, touch/stylus selection, autoscroll, resize handles or manual/native-input validation.

**Sample image color** is off initially and after a document revision, detachment or view-model change. Enable it and left-click an image pixel to show its decoded straight RGB and alpha bytes and a selectable, copyable `#AARRGGBB` value. The last color remains selected across document changes; no color is selected initially. Escape turns sampling off after the image has focus. Sampling and drag-crop modes are exclusive: enabling either disables the other, and switching during a crop drag cancels and restores its previous inputs. A click outside the image does nothing. Image-local coordinates are floored to pixels, with right and bottom edges excluded; zoom, scroll and bitmap DPI do not change the mapping. Sampling does not edit pixels, crop coordinates, history, dirty state or PNG output. It does not sample the checkerboard, preview, screen composite or other applications, and is currently mouse-only.

The editable **Color draft** starts at `#FF000000` (opaque black), but is not selected until explicitly applied; until then the brush does nothing. Enter exactly `#AARRGGBB`: a hash followed by eight ASCII hexadecimal digits, case-insensitive, with no whitespace. AA is alpha (`00` transparent, `FF` opaque). Invalid drafts show an error and applying them retains both the draft and the last valid selection. Press Enter in the draft or use the keyboard-focusable **Apply color** button; applying normalizes the draft to uppercase. A successful sample always replaces the draft and clears its error, even if the sampled color is unchanged; an out-of-bounds sample does neither. The read-only selected hex and RGB/alpha summary always show the actual brush color, separately from any uncommitted draft. Selection and draft persist across document changes and work without a document. Applying or sampling changes no pixels, dirty state, history, crop or PNG. The error is visible and exposed as the draft field's automation help text; native assistive-technology behavior has not been verified.

Sampling requires Avalonia's mouse `PointerPressed` event to report `PointerUpdateKind.LeftButtonPressed`, not merely a held left button. Right and middle presses while left is held do not sample. If a backend reports an unspecified or different update kind for a left press, that event is ignored rather than guessed; native physical input on every backend has not been verified.

**Brush** is explicitly selected and initially off. First sample or apply a color; without one the brush does nothing. It is mutually exclusive with drag crop and sampling. The keyboard-editable **Brush / eraser / line size** is shared, defaults to 1, and accepts only one or two ASCII digits representing 1–16. Invalid drafts display an error, prevent a new stroke, and never modify pixels or history. Changing the draft during a gesture cancels that gesture; changing it does not cancel a fill.

Press left inside the image, move and release to paint, or click to stamp. Image-local coordinates follow zoom and scroll and are independent of bitmap DPI and display scaling. A direction-independent Bresenham line joins floored pixel centers. At every center a square stamp of side `size` has offsets `-floor(size/2)` through `size-1-floor(size/2)` on each axis; even sizes therefore extend one pixel farther left and up. Stamps are clipped at all four edges, including when a captured pointer moves outside. At size 1 the previous one-pixel geometry is retained. Non-finite coordinates or magnitudes above one billion image units cancel the gesture. Mouse events must explicitly identify left-button press and release; unspecified and other button events are not guessed. Brush input is mouse-only, without pressure, antialiasing or autoscroll.

The stroke is buffered without a live preview and committed on left release as one undo step, using the selected color and size captured on press. Every covered pixel is composited at most once per gesture, even when revisited. Zero-alpha or otherwise unchanged strokes create no history and retain redo. Escape, capture loss, tool, size draft or selected-color change, view-model change, detachment, document replacement or a successful competing pixel operation cancels the pending stroke without restoring or overwriting another actor's state. Editing an uncommitted color draft or applying an invalid or unchanged color does not cancel it. During the gesture PNG, pixels, dirty state and history still describe the pre-stroke document; after release they describe the committed result. An external save during a gesture therefore saves only the pre-stroke revision.

**Eraser** is explicitly activated and initially off, and excludes brush, sampling and drag crop. It needs no sampled color and uses the same size, mouse-only coordinate mapping, clipping, Bresenham geometry, buffered release, cancellation and single-step undo as the brush. Covered pixels become transparent BGRA zero, including partially transparent pixels; already transparent strokes are no-ops and retain redo. An ambiguous release or another press during an eraser gesture cancels it rather than guessing. There is no live preview, layer support, touch/stylus guarantee or autoscroll.

**Line** is explicitly selected and mutually exclusive with the other modes. Select a color and valid size, press left inside the image and release at the endpoint. During drag a non-interactive black/yellow guide joins the start and current pixel centers, clipped to the image; a marker also shows a click. It is a position hint, not a pixel-accurate preview of the sized stamps or alpha composition, and remains visible even for transparent paint (which produces no change). It follows zoom and scroll, is independent of bitmap DPI and is never part of the image, history or PNG. Only the release endpoint determines the stroke. Both coordinates are floored to image pixels, then the existing clipped, direction-independent Bresenham segment and square stamps are used. A click stamps once; release outside is clipped, and non-finite or greater-than-one-billion coordinates cancel. Covered pixels are composited once per gesture and a changed result is one undo step; transparent or unchanged results retain dirty state and redo. The guide disappears on release, including a no-op, or when Escape, capture loss, ambiguous buttons, another press, tool, size or selected-color change, document/view-model change, detachment or competing edits cancel the gesture. Selecting Line cancels a pending fill. Zoom, scroll, display scaling and bitmap DPI use image-local coordinates. Only synthetic Avalonia mouse input has been tested; there is no physical-input, touch/stylus, antialiasing, pressure, autoscroll or visual-accessibility guarantee.

The gesture uses bitsets for visited centers and covered pixels, bounded by the image dimensions plus at most 15 pixels on each axis. A center is stamped at most once; a stamp performs at most 256 pixel checks. The final image copy and composition scan are synchronous and can pause the UI, especially for large images or long gestures. The bitmap revision and retained undo history require additional memory.

**Fill** is initially off and mutually exclusive with brush, eraser, sampling and drag crop. Select a color by sampling or applying the draft, then click a pixel with the left mouse button. Press and release must identify the left button, occur within the same pixel, and have no other mouse buttons held; ambiguous events, moving to another pixel, Escape, capture loss, tool or selected-color changes, detachment, document or view-model changes and competing edits cancel the gesture. Zoom, scroll, display scaling and bitmap DPI are handled by the image-local coordinate transform; right and bottom edges are excluded. Fill is mouse-only, with no preview.

Fill replaces the four-connected region whose decoded premultiplied BGRA bytes exactly equal the clicked pixel. Diagonal contact alone does not connect regions. It uses the selected straight color converted to premultiplied BGRA by `(channel × alpha + 127) / 255`, including zero-alpha transparent replacement; it does not composite like the brush. It has no tolerance, antialiasing, layer support or color management. A byte-identical result leaves dirty state, history and redo untouched; otherwise the completed operation is one undo step.

Traversal runs in UI-dispatcher background-priority chunks, yielding after at most 4,096 pixels or a roughly 4 ms checked budget. This is not a real-time guarantee: scheduling, allocation, the initial whole-image bitmap snapshot and final bitmap creation/copy and notification still run synchronously on the UI thread and can pause it. Nothing is published until the whole region is ready; while filling, pixels, dirty state, history and PNG export describe the previous revision. The status shows filling, completion or cancellation; Escape or another click cancels the pending fill (click again to start another). Undo/redo, a changed selected color or tool, crop gesture, document replacement, clear/dispose, detachment, a saved-revision change or a competing edit also cancel it. A stale queued chunk cannot commit. Only one fill may be active process-wide; another editor reports that it must retry.

The algorithm is O(pixel count) in time and memory. At the 4,194,304-pixel limit, the pixel array and queue are up to 16 MiB each; temporary/result bitmaps can add up to about 32 MiB, excluding source, history, rendering, GC retention and allocation overhead. Cancelled arrays become eligible for collection, not necessarily immediately reclaimed. The image limits are not a total memory guarantee.

The shared control displays fill errors. A failure before publication leaves the previous revision and redo unchanged; if a notification throws after publication, the revision may already be committed and the error says the image may have changed. The history-owned bitmap is not disposed by the failing fill, and busy state is released; a reentrant newer fill is not cancelled by the older failure. Normal .NET multicast semantics apply: if an application subscriber throws, later subscribers may not be notified, even when a revision has already committed. Errors after the control detaches or changes view model, and errors in the status display itself, are forwarded to diagnostic trace on a best-effort basis instead. Dispatcher callbacks do not create unobserved tasks.

In the host, open a PNG, JPEG or BMP, rotate or flip horizontally or vertically, undo/redo and select **Export PNG to new file**. The host asks before replacing or closing a dirty document. Clear is disabled in this host. Save/export is PNG only, including for JPEG and BMP inputs; original JPEG/BMP formats are never written. **PNG re-encoding may discard metadata**, color profiles, EXIF orientation and other ancillary information. It is not a metadata-preserving conversion. The PNG export is available even without editing and *always re-encodes*, including on an unmodified original PNG.

For open, the host captures the target document identity before invoking the picker. If the editor is loaded, cleared or disposed in the meantime, the pending open is canceled rather than replacing the new state. Edits to the same document while picking or reading are included in the subsequent dirty check. A discard confirmation is valid only for the exact document, current history revision and saved revision presented when the confirmation started; if any differs on return, open is canceled and must be retried. Undo back to the same revision is considered the same state. Picker cancellation, declined confirmation, a canceled confirmation task, and failed reads or decoding do not replace the image. This is UI-thread coordination, not external file-change detection or cancellation of the storage provider's own I/O.

## Integration contract

`Avln_ImageEditor.Controls` does not reference either the standalone host or any IDE. Create `ImageEditorViewModel`, bind it to `ImageEditorControl`, and load using either existing `LoadDocument(byte[], string)` or `LoadDocument(ImageEditorDocument)`. The document overload transfers ownership on success. A successful load replaces the document, clears history, starts clean and resets zoom; an invalid load does not replace the current document. Assigning `ActiveDocument` also loads or clears it. The host must ask about unsaved changes **before** calling either load overload, assigning `ActiveDocument`, or clearing/disposing. Set `AllowClear = false` when the enclosing host owns document state. Its default is true; the command, direct `ClearDocument()` and a null `ActiveDocument` assignment honor it. `Dispose()` always releases the document.

Use the view model and callbacks on the Avalonia UI thread, without reentrant mutations from event/property-change handlers. Treat the owned document and exposed image as read-only: external mutation of a caller-supplied bitmap bypasses the editor's history, dirty tracking and event.

* `ContentChanging` is raised immediately before an actual pixel-state change by brush, eraser, crop, rotation, horizontal or vertical flip, color inversion, grayscale conversion, undo or redo, not for load, clear, zoom, rename, export or a no-op edit.
* `InvertColors()` and `InvertColorsCommand` invert the current image without changing dimensions or alpha; the command is disabled without a document. A byte-identical result is a no-op and retains redo. An actual inversion is one history step and does not save.
* `ConvertToGrayscale()` and `ConvertToGrayscaleCommand` convert the current image without changing dimensions or alpha; the command is disabled without a document. A byte-identical result is a no-op and retains redo. An actual conversion is one history step and does not save.
* `CropX`, `CropY`, `CropWidth` and `CropHeight` are editable string properties. They parse as invariant-culture 32-bit whole numbers; `CropError` explains invalid input. `ApplyCropCommand.CanExecute(null)` is true only for a valid in-bounds rectangle with a loaded image and no active drag; `ApplyCrop()` also validates on every direct call. A successful crop raises `ContentChanging` and adds one undo state. A whole-image crop is enabled but is a no-op. The reset rules described above also apply to programmatic use.
* `ResetCropCoordinatesCommand` and `ResetCropCoordinates()` restore only these fields to `(0, 0, current width, current height)`; the command is disabled and the direct call is a no-op without a document. Neither affects pixels, revision, dirty state, undo/redo or PNG output.
* `CropPreview` reports the validated pixel rectangle (or an empty rectangle when invalid); `ShowCropPreview` is false for invalid or whole-image rectangles. The control draws it as a purely visual, non-hit-testable overlay; its outline straddles the specified pixel edges.
* `SamplePixel(x, y)` samples a zero-based in-bounds pixel and returns true; without a document or outside its bounds it returns false and retains the selection and draft. `SampledColor` is the current selection, nullable until the first sample or successful `ApplyManualColor()`; `SampledColorSummary` and `SampledColorHex` provide display text. `ManualColorHex` is the editable draft and `ManualColorError` describes invalid input. Sampling and applying are independent of undo/redo and do not raise `ContentChanging`. The control's sampling mode is view-local, not a paint tool.
* `Image` remains the owned bitmap; `DisplayImage` is its non-owning, pixel-coordinate view used by the shared control. Neither should be mutated or used after the owning document is disposed.
* `IsDirty` compares the current history state to the last saved state. Undo back to the saved state becomes clean; redo/other edits become dirty. History retains up to nine states including the current state; new edits after undo discard redo. State identity, not pixel-by-pixel comparison with the saved state, determines dirty status.
* `CapturePngSnapshot()` throws if no document is loaded or encoding fails; otherwise it **always encodes the displayed pixels** to PNG. `ImageEditorSaveSnapshot.Bytes` returns a defensive copy. It is bound to the document identity and history revision captured at that instant.
* `IsCurrentDocument(snapshot)` checks that identity, including after edits but not after clear, load or disposal. On the UI thread, check it immediately before assigning a completed Save-As path; do not compare `ActiveDocument` references, which change on undo/redo/rotation.
* `CaptureDocumentState()` captures identity, current history revision and saved revision without encoding. `IsSameDocument(state)` compares identity, including an unchanged empty editor; `IsCurrentDocumentState(state)` additionally compares both revisions. Check immediately before applying an asynchronous destructive operation. These methods do not track external bitmap mutation or file changes.
* Only after completely and successfully writing those exact bytes call `MarkSaved(snapshot)`. A later edit remains dirty; undoing back to that captured revision makes it clean. A snapshot from a replaced/cleared document is ignored. Never modify the byte copy before writing.
* After a successful Save As, `RenameDocument(snapshot, name)` updates the summary without a pixel mutation or dirty change. The snapshot guards document identity, so a replacement document cannot be renamed. It can rename the same document even if its revision changed during writing. Name must be nonempty.

The library deliberately performs no file I/O or conflict handling; integrating hosts choose their own file-writing policy and handle errors. The host-specific public method `MainWindowViewModel.ExportPngToNewFileAsync(path)` creates an unpredictable temporary sibling exclusively, writes and closes it, then moves it to the new local `.png` destination without overwrite before marking/renaming the snapshot. On ordinary write errors, cancellation or move conflicts it deletes its temporary sibling and never marks the snapshot saved. An abrupt process termination, external interference or a cleanup failure can still leave a temporary file; cleanup failures are reported. This is not a host-neutral file service. Picker export rejects nonlocal paths. Existing filenames or other I/O failures are reported in the host status. External processes can still rename, replace or delete paths during/after writing; this is not external-change detection, durable-fsync, or a race-free guarantee. Pick a different new filename to export again.

## Decode and pixel limits

Encoded input is restricted to PNG, JPEG and BMP headers with at most 32 MiB of encoded data, dimensions from 1 through 8192 each, and at most 4,194,304 pixels. `ImageDecodeLimits.ValidateEncoded` checks the PNG IHDR, JPEG first SOF, or BMP DIB declared dimensions before `new Bitmap(stream)` invokes the Avalonia decoder; decoded bitmap dimensions are checked too. Preflight rejects malformed/unsupported headers and over-limit inputs with `InvalidDataException`. Decoder errors propagate with platform-dependent exception types and leave the old document intact. This is not a complete integrity check: the platform decoder decides whether malformed/truncated data is acceptable. The standalone host checks seekable stream length before reading; for nonseekable streams it buffers at most the limit and reads at most one extra byte to detect overflow. A storage provider may perform its own I/O before returning a stream. The preflight limits declared geometry and input size, **not all native decoder allocations or CPU time**, nor behavior on contradictory malicious headers; the platform decoder is not a security sandbox for hostile files. Caller-supplied bitmap documents have already been decoded before this library can check their dimensions.

Crop, rotation, both flips, inversion and grayscale work on 8-bit BGRA premultiplied-alpha pixels. Inversion replaces each premultiplied color byte `c` with `alpha - c`. Grayscale uses BT.601 luma weights on encoded premultiplied channels: `(299 × R + 587 × G + 114 × B + 500) / 1000`, integer division, setting all three channels to the result. Both clamp invalid input channels above alpha, preserve alpha and keep transparent pixels transparent. These are not color-managed or linear-light operations. Premultiplication, decoding and PNG encoding can quantize colors, especially at low alpha; RGB hidden under zero alpha cannot be recovered, and exact original straight-RGB bytes are not guaranteed. Crop copies the rectangle without interpolation. Original color profiles, EXIF and other metadata are not preserved by these edits/export; decoder and encoder behavior varies by platform and no further framework or file-format guarantees are made. Decoding/encoding and a bounded undo history use additional memory; limits are not a total-process memory guarantee.

Sampling uses the same decoded 8-bit premultiplied BGRA representation as editing. For alpha `a > 0`, each channel is clamped to `a` and converted to straight RGB by integer `(channel × 255 + a / 2) / a`; at zero alpha the reported RGB is zero. Rounding and decoding can lose original channel values, especially at low alpha, and invisible source RGB cannot be recovered. The reported hex is this representable value, not guaranteed original file bytes. Sampling copies the decoded image into a temporary bitmap, bounded by the existing image limits; it is not color-managed.

Brush composition is encoded-space source-over on decoded premultiplied BGRA bytes. For each straight selected channel `c` and source alpha `a`, `s = (c × a + 127) / 255`; source alpha itself is `a`. For each color and alpha byte, the result is `min(255, s + (destination × (255 − a) + 127) / 255)` with integer division. Thus transparent paint is a no-op, including over transparent pixels; rounding may lose color information. This is not linear-light or color-managed. Buffering and the new revision require additional memory within the existing image-size limits.
